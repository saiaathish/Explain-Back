The pump maintains different sodium and potassium concentrations across the cell membrane, because it actively drives ions against their concentration gradients instead of letting them diffuse. It moves three sodium ions out of the cell and two potassium ions into the cell during each transport cycle. It uses ATP and changes shape during the transport cycle, because the ATP phosphorylates the pump and that shape change turns the sodium-binding sites outward. Releasing the phosphate returns the pump to its original shape. Because three positive charges leave while only two enter, each cycle leaves a small net negative charge inside the cell. The resulting gradients support the resting membrane potential and electrical signaling.
